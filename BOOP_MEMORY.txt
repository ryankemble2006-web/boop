PURPOSE
-------

This is BOOP's durable project memory for future conversations and coding
sessions. Read it before planning or changing BOOP. It preserves not only what
the project currently is, but why it became that way.

This is a decision ledger, not a replacement for live evidence:

1. The user's current instruction wins.
2. Physical behaviour and the current Home Assistant state beat documentation.
3. BOOP_RULES.md contains the short operational guardrails.
4. BOOP_STATUS.md contains the current engineering whiteboard.
5. Approved specs, plans, tests and source define implementation details.
6. This file supplies the product history, intent and reasons connecting them.

Never put passwords, Home Assistant tokens, signing keys, private certificates,
recovery codes or other secrets in this file. Public fingerprints and the fact
that private signing material exists are enough.


THE SHORTEST TRUE DESCRIPTION
-----------------------------

BOOP began as a smart speaker inside a cute, ownable plastic robot and is still
heading toward that embodied product. It has expressive eyes, behaves as an
honest puppet rather than pretending to be alive, and works FOR its owner. Home
Assistant is its local smart-home spinal cord. Phones, walls and the Nvidia
Shield are useful BOOP bodies and proving grounds, not evidence that the original
robot quietly stopped mattering.


ORIGIN: THE CUTE PLASTIC ROBOT
------------------------------

The original proposition was deliberately easy to love and easy to explain:
put a genuinely useful smart speaker inside a cute plastic robot called BOOP.
Do not sell fear, an abstract AI appliance, or a black box that claims to be a
person. Sell something useful, funny, physical and obviously ownable.

The robot concept grew a head, belly/arms and a tracked base. Tracks were not
feature creep for their own sake. They made BOOP's attention physically mobile:
the eyes could turn toward things, BOOP could move into useful positions, and a
voice interface could gain visible manners and presence. The face was described
as "a cursor with manners". Eyes show where the system's attention is without
requiring fake consciousness.

The larger product idea became a BOOP family. Historic concepts included BOOP
Home, BOOP Wall, BOOP Staff Entry, BOOP Dock, BOOP Splash and BOOP & BEEP. These
names are evidence of the product exploration, not promises that every named
device must ship. The stable idea underneath them is that BOOP can inhabit more
than one appropriate body while retaining one identity and one account:

    New body. Same BOOP.
    Every BOOP is different. Every BOOP is still BOOP.

The user's relationship is with BOOP before it is with any particular shell.
Hardware gives that existing relationship a body.


WHY BOOP IS A PUPPET
--------------------

BOOP is not a second fictional personality living inside ChatGPT and is not to
claim sentience, loneliness, love, need or emotional dependence. BOOP is the
visible performance of useful machinery: expressive, funny and surprising while
the humans remain obviously in charge.

This is not a demand for sterile software. Puppetry is the permission to be
charming without lying about what the system is. Show the performance first and
the strings when they are useful. Physical privacy cues and plain explanations
should make the mechanism understandable without ruining the magic.

Do not manufacture loneliness or use emotional coercion to retain attention.
BOOP works FOR the user. The companion mark for compatible products is:

    Works FOR BOOP

It is not "Made for BOOP" and must not imply a proprietary smart-device network.
BOOP should work with normal devices through Home Assistant, not force the house
into another ecosystem.


THE HUMAN IDEAS BAKED INTO BOOP
-------------------------------

Curiosity has no target demographic. BOOP should invite children, adults,
disabled people, experts, beginners and bored people to investigate and make.
Accessibility is ordinary product quality, not a patronising special mode.
BSL, ASL and other sign languages are first-class interaction ideas. Capability
and responsibility can be the reward; compulsory points, badges and gamification
are not required.

The Russell principle:

    Make failure safe enough that curiosity wins.

Trust is part of the sandbox. Safety must not become reflexive distrust. A good
system creates a protected place in which a person can try something and then
says, in effect, "Come see."

The John Paxton method:

    Have a go. Write it down. Repeat.

Offer the minimum useful interjection and do not rob the discovery. "Dunno" can
be an honest and productive answer. When the user says "I'm actually stuck",
switch from hints to full expert depth. Safety-critical situations also justify
direct intervention.

The Filing Cabinet is the model for learning systems: broken things, useful
failures, odd hardware, repair notes and discoveries are preserved safely enough
to explore again. BOOP should help a human teacher, parent, maker or expert join
at the learner's current level, supply only the missing information, and hand the
work back. BOOP must not replace the garage, teacher or shared moment.

Whimsy belongs above a serious foundation. A bug that creates harmless play may
become folklore; a bug that destroys trust, data, safety or agency gets fixed.
Do not make BOOP boring merely because predictability is easier to specify.


OPEN, OWNABLE AND REPAIRABLE
----------------------------

The physical BOOP should be understandable and repairable. Prefer screws,
standard fasteners, common connectors and replaceable modules. Avoid glue, heat
guns, gratuitous Torx and sealed assemblies used only to deny access.

Open hardware does not mean putting a Raspberry Pi in a shell and declaring the
problem solved. A Pi may be a BOOP Staff/development tool, but it is not the
default consumer brain merely because it is familiar to makers. The foundation
must be safe and reliable; the playground above it should remain open.

A BOOP Staff device was imagined as a helpful specialist that advertises its
capabilities plainly - for example, "I'm a lamp. Come find me" - and can use a
look-at-the-thing handshake instead of serial-number and button-combination
rituals. Preserve the principle even if the eventual hardware differs.


PRODUCT EVOLUTION AND THE REASONS FOR IT
----------------------------------------

1. Smart-speaker robot concept

   BOOP started as a useful speaker with an expressive robot body. The name and
   face made advanced computing approachable without disguising its nature.
   Mobility, tracks, docks and accessories explored what a physical BOOP could
   do around a home.

2. Product family and accessibility exploration

   Wall, Staff, portable/wall-mount and sign-language ideas expanded BOOP beyond
   one chassis. The reason was not to fragment the brand; it was to let the same
   BOOP relationship inhabit bodies suited to different rooms, people and jobs.
   Historic family names remain concepts until deliberately revived.

3. Home Assistant as the local spinal cord

   Home Assistant was chosen because it already knows the house: entities,
   areas, integrations, permissions, state and automations. BOOP must not copy
   that database or grow a second automation engine. Deterministic home control
   stays local and continues without cloud AI. Cloud intelligence may add
   language and charm after a local action, but cannot be required to turn on a
   light.

4. Pixel 7 Pro puppet-body Alpha

   A spare Pixel became the fastest available physical body for proving BOOP's
   eyes, tap-to-speak, speech, local HA control, room context, wake behaviour and
   puppetry. This was a pragmatic prototype: use excellent existing display,
   microphones, sensors and compute before custom plastic exists.

   Alpha work progressed through launch/speech hardening, real room-scoped HA
   actions, media control, Muppet-style expression, wake-word and shake-to-wake
   experiments, colour control and timed-command experiments. Physical tests
   proved important behaviour such as controlling only the intended room and
   changing only lights that were already on.

   Voice was substantially working in the last Alpha and must not be remembered
   as a failed or discarded feature. The remaining trouble was concentrated in
   the newer timed-command automatic follow-up path, especially repeated
   NO_MATCH/error 7 responses. Small patches relying on optional partial
   recognition were not a trustworthy fix for that edge. Future Alpha work may
   revisit and strengthen voice while preserving the behaviour that was already
   working on the physical Pixel.

5. Shield overlay proof of concept

   The Nvidia Shield offered a different experiment: let BOOP's eyes appear over
   the launcher, Kodi and video, as though she had escaped the phone to explore.
   This was explicitly puppetry. The first Shield build was disposable and
   isolated so it could answer one question without importing Pixel speech or HA
   complexity.

   The protected overlay therefore has strict boundaries: transparent eyes,
   non-focusable, non-touchable, no microphone, and no Home Assistant logic.
   The remote continues to control the app beneath it. HDR/display-mode fixes
   were made only to keep the overlay alive and visually stable.

6. Shield BOOP Home

   Once the overlay was viable, Shield became a useful living-room BOOP body and
   remote-first control surface. The project added secure local pairing, a BOOP
   Wall companion handoff, encrypted credential storage, room selection and a
   large TV dashboard. This did not replace the robot or Pixel. It proved a
   low-friction local setup model and a sofa-appropriate interface.

   The current pairing direction is QR-based and keeps credentials out of the QR
   payload. The successful Shield flow should later be brought back into the
   latest Pixel Alpha rather than restarting the Pixel puppet from Alpha 1.

7. Routines as one BOOP word

   Home Assistant exposes automations, scripts and scenes as different technical
   types. BOOP deliberately presents all three to a person as one thing:

       Routine

   This is a language decision, not a protocol shortcut. Internally each exact
   entity still uses the correct Home Assistant service and truthful completion
   rule. User-facing screens should not drift back to three competing nouns.

8. BOOP Wall resurrection after the Shield companion update

   The stable-signed BOOP Wall APK used for the newer Shield pairing companion
   carried an old echo-only MainActivity under the same com.boop.alpha1 package.
   Installing it updated the Pixel app in place, leaving eyes and speech echo but
   replacing the smart Alpha behaviour. The missing brain was not lost.

   The version history split after Alpha 6.5. Alpha 6.5.3 natural wake expanded
   the local detector to exactly 33 phrases, all mapped to the same BOOP event;
   Alpha 6.5.4 and 6.5.5 continued from it with customization fixes and
   shake/puppet work. The apparently newer 6.6.x timed-routine line forked before
   those changes and is not their superset. The correct resurrection base is
   alpha6.5.5-shake-muppet, not alpha6.6.3-scheduling-acknowledgement.

   Ordinary conversation in that Alpha was routed through Home Assistant to the
   OpenCode conversation agent, not through a direct OpenAI key in Android. On
   2026-09-05 the OpenCode token was found expired, the user re-authenticated it,
   and confirmed the current agent named BOOP answers again.

   The approved direction is to modernise the preserved Alpha with stable signing
   and secure QR pairing. Restore the full 33-phrase natural wake collection,
   normal conversation, immediate local HA control and direct media control.
   The user correctly remembered examples including "ey BOOP", "hey BOOP" and
   "hello BOOP"; Git commit 0724326 preserves the complete generated asset and
   tests. Do not collapse it back to the older single-keyword Alpha 6.4 asset.
   Deliberately remove timed voice routines from the new runtime because their
   follow-up flow needs a separate rethink. Do not create a physical checkpoint
   until all restored paths pass on the wall Pixel.


LOCKED PRODUCT AND ARCHITECTURE DECISIONS
-----------------------------------------

- Master user-facing brand: BOOP.
- BOOP is an honest puppet/interface, not a fake sentient companion.
- BOOP works FOR the user; compatible products may be labelled Works FOR BOOP.
- Home Assistant is the local authority and smart-home spinal cord.
- Basic control must work without OpenAI or any other cloud service.
- Do not duplicate HA entities, rooms, permissions or automation state.
- If HA exposes an entity to Assist/BOOP, BOOP may use it; do not bypass that
  boundary with a second BOOP permission model.
- Normal device control can execute immediately. Creating, changing or deleting
  a routine requires a plain-English read-back and explicit confirmation.
- Fail plainly. Never claim an action succeeded merely because it probably did.
- Never silently substitute a same-named device in another room.
- TV interaction is remote-first, chunky, high contrast and not touch-dependent.
- The protected Shield overlay contains eyes and puppetry only: no microphone,
  HA logic, focus capture or touch capture.
- Automations, scripts and scenes are all called Routines in BOOP-facing copy.
- Open/repairable hardware and physical privacy cues remain long-term values.
- Accessibility is first-class product design, not a separate lesser experience.
- Preserve working behaviour and checkpoints; make small evidence-backed changes.
- BOOP Wall ordinary conversation uses Home Assistant's BOOP OpenCode agent;
  Android must not contain a direct OpenAI credential.
- The correct BOOP Wall resurrection base is Alpha 6.5.5; do not assume the
  later-numbered 6.6.x timed branch contains its natural-wake improvements.
- Timed voice routines are excluded from the BOOP Wall resurrection and require
  a separate future redesign.
- CI green and physically green are different states and must be labelled as such.

HOW TO MAINTAIN THIS MEMORY
---------------------------

Update this file whenever any of these changes:

- a product principle is explicitly accepted, rejected or renamed;
- a prototype pivots and the reason matters;
- a feature crosses from planned to implemented, CI-green or physically green;
- a checkpoint becomes protected;
- a previous fact becomes false;
- a repeated conversation-memory failure reveals missing context.

Maintenance rules:

1. Read the current file before editing it.
2. Change the old statement instead of stacking a contradictory new one.
3. Preserve the reason behind a pivot, not only the latest outcome.
4. Date material reconciliations and evidence changes.
5. Explicitly label physical, CI, implementation and design-only claims.
6. Keep tentative ideas separate from locked decisions.
7. Do not promote an old assistant suggestion into a decision unless the user or
   later evidence accepted it.
8. Never record secrets.
9. Reconcile BOOP_STATUS.md when the current engineering state changes.
10. If this file disagrees with the user's current words or a physical test,
    correct this file.
